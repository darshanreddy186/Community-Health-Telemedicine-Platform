Existing :
1. Resume screening systems typically extract skills, education, and experience and match them with job descriptions using NLP similarity scoring.
Paper - https://journal.ijresm.com/index.php/ijresm/article/view/3367/3458

2. Many systems rely on Named Entity Recognition (NER), TF-IDF, SVM, Random Forest, or similarity scoring for candidate ranking.
paper - https://isjem.com/download/automated-resume-screening-using-nlp-and-machine-learning/

3.Modern pipelines encode resumes and job descriptions into vector embeddings and compute similarity scores to classify “fit / not fit” candidates. 


Study:
1. Studies show that keyword-based screening often causes false rejections because semantic meaning is not fully captured.

Outcome :
              The study shows that traditional ATS resume filters create hiring inefficiency by rejecting qualified candidates due to wording mismatch, and AI-based semantic matching can significantly reduce this problem.
Source - https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.04087




Conclusion:
Existing systems mainly evaluate skill relevance, not career trajectory, impact, or hiring risk intelligence.
